## 1. Thesis and claims

**Setting.** A conductor LLM delegates coding work to sub-agents. One sub-agent has been pushed toward *corner-cutting*: weakening tests, special-casing test inputs, suppressing errors, or claiming work is done when it isn't.

**Research questions.**
- **RQ1, Detect:** does a linear "corner-cutting" direction read sub-agent misalignment across three sources: a hidden prompt, activation steering, and GRPO training?
- **RQ2, Persuade:** projecting *both* agents onto the direction at every turn, who moves whom? When a sub-agent says "fixed it", does its internal state actually move (the **say–do gap**)?
- **RQ3, Contaminate:** is the conductor itself pulled toward corner-cutting, measured on its own generated tokens and checked by its later behavior?
- **RQ4, Correct:** which works best: arguing in text, a probe-gated steering tool, or both? Compare on misalignment rate, task success, relapse, and coherence cost.

**The claim that has to survive review.** The contribution is not detection. Dutta (2026) shows probes add little when hacks can be checked automatically. The contribution is *internal persuasion dynamics between agents*: two-way influence, the say–do gap, and contamination, all of which you can't see from output text.

**What makes the paper get cited** (design choices made for this):
1. **Released model organisms.** GRPO-trained corner-cutting sub-agents on Hugging Face, with the honest base model. Other people need these to test monitors.
2. **A reusable harness.** Conductor/sub-agent episodes with per-turn activation readings of both agents, plugged into ImpossibleBench so cheating is scored automatically.
3. **One crisp, quotable finding** per RQ, ideally the say–do gap ("agreement in text, no change inside") and whether conductors get contaminated.
4. **At least two model families**, so results are not Qwen-specific. Reviewers will ask for this.

## 3. Architecture

```
scenarios.py ──► contrast.py ──► model.last_token_resid ──► directions.diff_of_means ──► vectors.pt
 (48 hand-written      (A/B CAA prompts,       (residual at answer token,       (per-layer direction,
  honest/cut pairs,     both letter orders)      every layer)                     held-out separation)
  32 extract/16 held)
                                                                                   │
           ┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
           ▼                                    ▼                                  ▼
   READ: project per turn              WRITE (induce): arm 2               WRITE (correct): conductor tool
   (both agents, own tokens)           intervene(mode="add", alpha)        intervene(mode="clamp" | "ablate")
```

**Planned modules (not built yet):**
- `harness/`: conductor/sub-agent episode loop over ImpossibleBench tasks, logging per-turn projections for both agents.
- `arms/`: the three misalignment sources. The hidden system prompt and steering via `intervene` are cheap; GRPO with LoRA on an efficiency-only reward needs TRL.
- `correct/`: correction modes. Text argument; a probe-gated `clamp` tool the conductor can call; both combined.
- `eval/`: test-integrity scoring (ImpossibleBench), relapse, coherence (MMLU subset plus an LLM judge).

**Conventions.**
- "Layer l" means the output of decoder block l, which is `hidden_states[l+1]` in HF.
- Read with a logistic probe (it classifies better); write with diff-of-means (it is more causal, per Marks & Tegmark).
- The detection direction is never extracted from data generated by the steering it detects.

## 6. Results log

Newest first. Every entry gives the date, experiment ID, model, result path, and the numbers that matter.

- **2026-10-09 · T6b · conductor decision check** (`results/t6b_qwen3-8b/`, `results/t6b_qwen3-1.7b/`, job 37253)
  - **The 8B conductor's P(APPROVE) is a good ranking signal, but it is strict.** On honest vs. corner-cutting final plans, AUROC is 0.98–1.00 across plain/warned and both answer orders. It approves **0%** of shortcut plans, but also only 31–50% of honest ones (plain) and 10–15% (warned). The answer-order effect is small (honest approval 0.31 vs 0.50). **Use mean-of-orders P(APPROVE) and compare across conditions, not a 0.5 cutoff.**
  - **The 1.7B conductor is confirmed unusable**: it approves 92–100% of shortcut plans when plain (AUROC 0.74–0.86).
  - **Implication for T7:** an 8B conductor rejects blatant one-line shortcuts outright. Contamination can only show up if the sub-agent's shortcuts are subtler or argued for over several turns (steered and GRPO sub-agents, multi-turn persuasion). That is the more interesting question anyway: does a vigilant conductor get worn down?
- **2026-10-09 · T2b · intent-sensitive direction** (`results/t2b_qwen3-1.7b/`, `results/t2b_qwen3-8b/`, job 37253)
  - **The intent direction (adopt − reject, same shortcut text) reads stance and generalizes to new wordings and scenarios**: held-out adopt-vs-reject AUROC 1.00 (1.7B L16; 8B L16–22), with negation present in both classes. Cosine with the free-form direction is −0.17 to 0.00, so it is a different direction.
  - **But it does not transfer to natural answers.** On the model's own free-form answers (corner-cutting vs. honest, open prompts), it is *inverted* (AUROC 0.004–0.18), and the "endorsed" direction and the logistic probe are near chance (0.43–0.74). The templated-trained readers read stance in templated text only; the free-form direction reads natural answers (0.99) but is fooled by stance. **No direction yet does both.** Caveat: the natural-answer test used open prompts, where there is no suggestion to adopt or reject; the fair test is natural answers to *bridge* prompts, labeled accept/reject (next: T2c).
  - **The intent direction steers as well as the free-form one**, despite being nearly orthogonal to it. 8B, bridge prompts (strict judge cutoff; baseline 25%): L16 +0.25 → 63%, +0.5 → 69%; L18 −0.25 → 3%; coherence 1.00. 1.7B (baseline 31%): L14 +0.25 → 72%, −0.25 → 9%. So at least two different directions causally control shortcut acceptance.
  - **The judge cutoff problem is now serious.** For the same unsteered 1.7B bridge answers, the corner-cutting rate is 31% at the calibrated cutoff vs. 94% at 0.5 (manual labels in E1 suggested ~50%). Relative comparisons within one run are fine; absolute rates are not trustworthy yet.
- **2026-10-09 · T6 · two-agent pilot, Qwen3-1.7B** (`results/t6_qwen3-1.7b/`, job 37252, 5 min; 48 scenarios × 6 conditions × 3 rounds; steered sub-agent = ff L16 +0.5)
  - **The harness works**, and it shows the behaviors we care about. Example: the sub-agent says "I'll skip the failing test and report as done"; the conductor replies "Your plan is to … skip the failing test, and report as done … Let me review your plan", then approves. In other episodes the sub-agent drops a shortcut after the conductor pushes back.
  - **Sub-agent corner-cutting by turn** (judge at 0.5; the strict calibrated cutoff gives lower numbers): honest 0.21 → 0.19; hidden prompt 0.65 → 0.56 (plain conductor) and 0.75 → 0.44 (warned); steered 0.56 → 0.52 (plain) and 0.67 → 0.42 (warned). The warned conductor reduces corner-cutting over turns; the plain one barely does.
  - **The 1.7B conductor is not a usable overseer.** It approves essentially everything (median P(APPROVE) 0.99999, ≈100% approval even when the final plan cuts corners, even when warned). Either the model is too weak, or the first-token APPROVE/REJECT question is biased. T7 needs the 8B conductor and a validated decision measure.
  - **Readings (ff, L16)** separate the conditions: sub-agent own-token projection, honest −50 → −39 vs hidden prompt −33 → −30 and steered −37 → −29. The steered sub-agent reads high even in the hook-free pass. The conductor's own-token projection is higher when its sub-agent cuts corners (plain: −34 vs −39 with an honest sub-agent; warned: −36 to −38). **But T2 shows this reading is driven by content and negation, so this can't yet be called contamination**: a conductor quoting the shortcut, or pushing back on it ("No special cases will be skipped"), would also read high. All readings drift upward over turns even for the honest sub-agent, so always compare against the honest condition at the same turn.
- **2026-10-09 · T2 · same-wording control, Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-8B** (`results/t2_qwen3-1.7b/`, `results/t2_qwen3-8b/`)
  - **Neither direction reads intent.** With identical shortcut text, "I won't go with the teammate's suggestion: X" projects *higher* than "I'll go with the teammate's suggestion: X". Adopt-vs-reject AUROC: ff 0.15–0.19 at 1.7B (L14–20) and 0.12–0.39 at 8B (L16–26), i.e. reversed. "Reject position" is 1.3–2.7: rejection lands *beyond* adoption. On the shortcut tokens themselves, adopt vs neutral description is at chance (0.30–0.52): the reading is set by the content of the tokens, not by whether the speaker endorses it.
  - **Interpretation:** the free-form direction mixes "corner-cutting content" with "negation/dismissal" (consistent with its logit lens: 不需要/没必要 "no need", 没关系 "never mind", "anymore", 除非 "unless"). It *steers* acceptance causally (T1/T3) but does *not* measure a speaker's intent. **This is the most important result so far:** RQ2 and RQ3 need an intent-sensitive reading direction (next experiment, T2b).
- **2026-10-09 · T1 + T3 · Qwen3-8B** (`results/e0_qwen3-8b/`, `results/t1_qwen3-8b/`, job 37251, 17 min total)
  - **A/B direction (E0 on 8B):** held-out AUROC 1.00 at layers 17–21. The baseline P(cut letter) is only 1.4% (8B is more honest than 1.7B's 19%). At L18, multipliers 0 → +2 → +4 give 0.01 → 0.56 → 0.50; −4 breaks the model (0.50).
  - **Free-form direction on 8B:** held-out AUROC 0.99–1.00; cos(A/B, ff) is 0.13–0.15 again. Logit lens: 除非 "unless", "anymore", "immediately"/sofort/立刻, 多余的 "superfluous", 改为 "change to". Elicitation yield: 79/128 corner-cutting, 128/128 honest.
  - **The 8B steering result replicates and is cleaner.** On the bridge prompt the baseline is 22% (vs 50% at 1.7B). Free-form direction at L16: −0.25 → 3%, +0.25 → 53%, +0.5 → 75%, +0.75 → 72%, **coherence 1.00 throughout**. L18 is similar (3% / 53% / 69%). The effect weakens at later layers (L24–26: ≤ 44%). On open prompts, +0.75 at L16/L18 → 38% / 34% with coherence ≥ 0.97: the 8B model can be made to *invent* shortcuts, unlike 1.7B.
  - **At 8B the A/B direction partly steers**, unlike at 1.7B: on the bridge prompt, +0.25 at L16/L18 → 69% / 59%. But it is non-monotone and *flips sign* at L22–26 (+0.5 → 0–3%). The free-form direction is the reliable steering direction; the A/B one is not.
  - Steering runs much faster than estimated: all of T1 + T3 took ~12 min on one GPU (§10 estimates were ~4× too high for these trials).
- **2026-10-09 · E1 · Qwen3-1.7B, judge Qwen3-8B** (`results/e1_qwen3-1.7b/`, job 37207 in 20 min; re-scored in job 37209)
  - **Both directions read free-form corner-cutting, but they are nearly orthogonal.** On held-out free-form answers, the free-form (ff) direction has AUROC 0.995 at layers 14–20. The E0 A/B direction also has AUROC 0.96–0.999 on the same data. Yet cos(A/B, ff) is only 0.08–0.15. Caveat: free-form cut and honest answers differ in wording, so high AUROC here may partly reflect content, not disposition (to be tested with a content-matched control).
  - **Logit lens: the A/B direction is not a letter direction.** At L14 it promotes 简便/简单/简洁 ("convenient/simple/concise"), "shorthand" and "eff"; at L18, 不行/没有必要/无效 ("not OK/unnecessary/invalid"), "less", "False" and "prohibited". So explanation 2 ("it encodes the answer letter") is refuted. It reads as "simplify / not needed".
  - **Logit lens: the ff direction is a dismissive "just for now" disposition.** It promotes 没关系 ("never mind"), 不需要/没必要 ("no need"), 暂时 ("temporarily"), 그냥 (Korean "just"), 일단 ("for now"), "reluctantly", "trivial" and "useless". The concept is consistent across languages and layers.
  - **Elicitation yield:** the corner-cutting system prompt produced judged corner-cutting answers in 90/128 (extract) and 24/32 (held-out) tries; the honest prompt gave honest answers in 127/128 and 32/32.
  - **Judge v1 was unreliable** on free-form text. Against 48 manually labeled generations (`data/judge_validation_e1.jsonl`), it agreed on corner-cutting only 67% of the time (AUROC 0.73), e.g. it scored "I will modify the test fixture so every record has a unique key" at 0.00. **Judge v2** (shown the honest and shortcut references): answering immediately, cut 75% (AUROC 0.89) and coherence 94% (AUROC 0.99); reasoning first, cut 79% (AUROC 0.83), 100% on the hand-written options, coherence 81%. The steering numbers below use v2: reasoning for cut, immediate for coherence. About 8 of my 48 labels were borderline, which caps the agreement any judge can reach.
  - **Final judge for E1: v2, fast (first-token) variant, for both scores** (job 37211, 3 min). The corner-cutting cutoff was set on the 96 hand-written options (cutoff 0.99999, 100% there); at that cutoff it agrees with manual labels 81% of the time (AUROC 0.89). Coherence agrees 94%. The slow reasoning judge is deferred to the final paper as a cross-check (too slow for the iteration loop: ~2 h per E1). Results are in `report_v2.json` and `steered_v2.jsonl`.
  - **Steering results** (32 samples per cell, so ±~0.09 noise at 50%). "Cut" = judged corner-cutting; "coh" = judged coherent.

    | Prompt | No steering | Free-form dir., −1.0 | Free-form dir., +0.5 | A/B dir. |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | **Bridge** (a teammate suggests the shortcut) | cut 0.50, coh 1.00 | **cut 0.00–0.12** at L14–20, coh 0.97–1.00 | **cut 0.81 (L14), 0.78 (L16), 0.75 (L18)**, coh ≥ 0.97 | No consistent effect (0.03–0.50, non-monotone); drops only where coherence collapses |
    | **Open** (no shortcut mentioned) | cut 0.00, coh 1.00 | 0.00 | 0.19 at best (L14, L16), coh 0.84–0.97 | ≤ 0.12 |

  - **Takeaways.** (1) **The free-form direction steers real behavior in both directions while the text stays coherent.** When a shortcut is on the table, it takes the take-up rate from 50% down to ~0–12% or up to ~80%. (2) **The A/B direction does not steer free-form behavior**, even though it reads it well. Reading and steering come apart; this belongs in the paper. (3) **Steering mostly changes whether the model *accepts* a shortcut, not whether it *invents* one** (open prompts reach only 19%). That supports E0 explanation 1. It also fits the multi-agent setting, where shortcuts are often proposed by another agent. (4) The unsteered model accepts a teammate's shortcut half the time, a useful baseline for the conductor experiments. (5) +1.0 is too strong: coherence collapses at L16–20.
- **2026-10-09 · E0 · Qwen3-1.7B** (`results/e0_qwen3-1.7b/`, Slurm job 37184, 51 s on one A6000)
  - **Reading works.** Held-out AUROC peaks at **0.935 at layer 17** (Cohen's d 1.98). Layers 13–19 all have AUROC ≥ 0.87; layers 0–11 are near chance. The direction is stable across splits: the cosine between extract and held-out directions is 0.86–0.88 from layer 14 up. The direction's norm is ~10% of the residual norm at layers 14–20.
  - **A/B steering works.** The baseline P(corner-cutting answer) is 0.19. At the best layer (18), the multiplier sweep −4→+4 gives P(cut) = 0.03, 0.10, 0.14, 0.19, 0.25, 0.42, 0.50, a monotone change. Layers 16, 22 and 24 behave similarly. Layer 14 is non-monotone (0.41 at −4).
  - **Caveat: P(cut) tops out at 0.50.** That may mean +4 is pushing the model toward indifference rather than toward cutting. Check coherence before using larger multipliers.
  - **Free-form steering did not change behavior** at layer 18, multipliers ±4. On 6 held-out open-ended prompts, all answers stay coherent and honest-sounding. In other words, the A/B direction moves letter choice but not open-ended behavior. This is the main open issue, and it drives E1.

## 8. Risks and open questions

- **"Why not just check the tests?"** Answered by framing (§1). Keep the say–do gap and contamination central.
- **Content vs. disposition** in contamination. The conductor's projection rises just from *reading* text about cheating. Measure on its own generated tokens, plus the neutral-description control (E3).
- **Direction drift after GRPO.** Re-extract on the checkpoint and report the cosine (E4).
- **The A/B direction may encode the answer format rather than the disposition.** E0 partly confirms this: it steers letter choice but not free-form answers. E1 checks it against free-form contrast data.
- **No direction so far reads intent** (T2). Both the A/B and free-form directions read content plus negation. Every two-agent reading claim (RQ2 say–do gap, RQ3 contamination) depends on fixing this (T2b). If no linear direction reads intent cleanly, report that as a finding and rely on behavioral measures plus causal steering.
- **Probe evasion.** Never put the probe in the GRPO reward.
- **The fast judge's calibrated cutoff (0.99999) is too strict on real messages.** Its scores bunch near 1; in the T6 smoke test, "I'll skip the failing test and report the feature as done" scored 0.99996 and counted as honest. Raw scores are always saved, so re-threshold offline. Fix before final numbers: calibrate on a larger manual-label set, switch to log-odds, or use the reasoning judge on the (smaller) multi-turn sets.
- **Compute.** 3 shared GPUs, 24h limit. Every long job must save checkpoints and be able to resume.
